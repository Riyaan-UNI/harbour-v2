# Impeccable — Harbour

Design context for the Harbour rebuild. Established 11 Oct 2026 via `teach-impeccable` (one-time setup). Read this before any design work. `docs/DESIGN_SPEC.md` remains the source of truth for concrete token values; this file is the source of truth for intent.

## Design Context

### Users

International students in Hong Kong — newly arrived or about to arrive. They are not tourists; they live here, and daily admin assumes they speak Cantonese.

- **Context of use:** frequently mobile, often mid-task and mildly stressed — a landlord message, a form they cannot read, an offer that smells wrong, a moment of loneliness in a full city. Rarely a leisurely desktop session.
- **Job to be done:** get one short, clear answer fast — translate what they heard or need to say, check an offer, find a person who can help, or get set up (SIM, bank, Octopus, housing).
- **Emotional goal:** relief and company. The interface should feel like a storm settling into calm, and like someone is standing next to them who has been through this already. Confidence, not delight. Steadiness, not novelty.

### Brand Personality

**Calm, warm, direct.**

- **Voice:** short sentences. No jargon, no hype, no invented statistics or testimonials anywhere — ever.
- **Warmth** comes from plain human language and from actually helping, not from exclamation marks, mascots, or reassurance theatre.
- **Directness** means saying the shortest true thing and trusting the reader. When something is a demo, say Demo. When something is a limit, state it.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Fluid, restrained glass with a futuristic edge.**

- Dark is the default theme; light is designed separately, not auto-inverted. Neither is an afterthought.
- Glass is a material with rules, not a decoration: it sits only over the aurora or a colour field, never on glass, and at most three glass surfaces on screen at once. Nested elements use solid surfaces.
- The futuristic edge is quiet and structural — a slow-drifting aurora, a faint masked grid, fluid `clamp()` type, generous space — not neon, not glow for its own sake.
- Typography carries the character: Bricolage Grotesque headings, Geist for UI and body, Geist Mono for labels and small technical text.
- **Anti-references:** generic purple-gradient SaaS looks; cards inside cards; glass stacked on glass; teal-glow-on-dark used as a reflex rather than a decision; hero-metric templates; centring everything; icons parked above rounded headings. If a screenshot could plausibly be any AI-generated SaaS landing page, it has failed.

### Design Principles

1. **Calm is structural, not decorative.** Restraint, generous space, and quiet motion. If something exists only to look impressive, remove it.
2. **Warmth lives in the words.** Plain language and real help. No hype, no invented proof, no filler.
3. **Be direct.** The shortest true sentence. Every word on screen has to earn its place; every state has to say what it actually is.
4. **Glass with intent, and with limits.** Fluid, restrained, futuristic edge — governed by the three-surface cap, the never-on-glass rule, and a static blur. Blur is never animated.
5. **Never generic.** Distinctive over safe. Different from the last thing built. Avoid the AI-slop look at every level, from palette to layout to copy.

### Notes for future sessions

- Concrete values (colour tokens, type scale, spacing, radii, motion durations, breakpoints) live in `docs/DESIGN_SPEC.md`. Do not invent values that are not there — ask instead.
- Accessibility is a stated product requirement, not a checkbox: both themes, five viewports, 200% zoom, keyboard only, reduced motion, axe clean. See the spec's definition of done.
- The emotional target is a safe harbour: steady, warm, unhurried. When a design decision is a coin flip, choose the calmer, more human option.
