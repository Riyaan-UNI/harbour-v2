# Harbour motion and visual inspiration

> **Note for anyone cloning this repo:** the reference videos and images this document describes live in `inspiration/`, which is git-ignored (about 70 MB). They are kept on the maintainer's machine only. This file is the written record of what was taken from them, and it stands on its own.

This document turns the eight supplied MP4 references into a Harbour-specific visual and motion plan. The videos were opened from the shared SharePoint folder and reviewed in its preview. Seven rendered and were inspected at multiple points. `landing-page-inspiration2.mp4` remained black in the SharePoint preview, so its contents are deliberately marked unverified below. Its filename and file size are not evidence of what it depicts.

The aim is to carry over the references' visual techniques and energy while making every scene about Harbour: an international student's first weeks in Hong Kong, clear guidance, privacy, language support and scam awareness. Use the concepts as references, not as source artwork. Do not copy their logos, characters, headlines, exact layouts, or branded illustrations.

## Reference-by-reference review

### 1. `landingpage-insp.mp4` — financial-services landing page

**Observed.** A deep emerald surround frames a light web page. The hero uses a large editorial headline, including a contrasting italic phrase, over a dark field with a dotted globe and floating data chips. Scrolling moves through a compact dashboard/bento section, a dark KPI area with rising bars, and a final call to action. Green is the main signal color; white and charcoal surfaces keep the information dense but readable. The type pairing is a clean sans for interface copy with a more expressive italic display treatment for emphasis.

**Energy and motion.** Confident, precise, and data-led. The page camera scrolls and pans; content is revealed in editorial chapters. The globe and chips create a sense of activity without making the interface itself chaotic.

**Harbour use.** Use the chapter pacing for the public landing page: student problem → Harbour tools → examples → next step. Replace money, currencies, and company metrics with clearly labelled product samples such as checklist progress and example scam signals. Use a journey map or Hong Kong location motif instead of a finance globe. Any sample must remain visibly marked `SAMPLE` or `DEMO`.

### 2. `landing-page-inspiration1.mp4` — travel/editorial landing page

**Observed.** A saturated cobalt-blue canvas alternates with large white editorial panels. Oversized high-contrast serif lettering, including an italic phrase, sits above travel imagery. Further down, image-led sections tell a story about places and experiences before closing on a dark-blue footer.

**Energy and motion.** Cinematic and expansive rather than dashboard-dense. Slow page movement, large image crops, and alternating light/dark sections make the scroll feel like a guided journey.

**Harbour use.** Borrow the sense of arrival and the strong chapter changes for Harbour's landing page and onboarding. Replace destination marketing with real student moments—first MTR trip, first rental viewing, asking a question in Cantonese. Use photographs or original illustrations that Harbour owns or can license. Keep text over imagery short and provide a solid, high-contrast text surface wherever body copy or controls sit.

### 4. `loading-ui-video-conceptartanimationsinsp.mp4` — comic-book title sequence

**Observed.** Dark frames are masked by oversized `MEW` lettering, then the scene shifts into a saturated red-and-black comic composition with a large title, illustrated cats, panels, and action imagery. The palette is red, black, and white with a deliberately loud graphic contrast. Display lettering is heavy and condensed; supporting text is secondary.

**Energy and motion.** Playful, punchy, and deliberately theatrical. Large type masks, quick cuts, panel movement, and character reveals make the loading moment feel like an opening title sequence.

**Harbour use.** Adapt the *reveal grammar* for a celebratory checklist completion or first-arrival welcome: a bold Harbour wordmark or abstract map marker reveals a small original illustration. Keep it brief and skippable. Do not use the cats, `MEW` lettering, or the source's recognizable composition. Never put this sequence in front of an urgent safety action.

### 5. `loading-ui-video-conceptartanimationsinsp2.mp4` — restrained sci-fi progress screen

**Observed.** A near-black interface has sparse star/grid or circuit-like details, a small violet motif, and blocky monospaced display text that appears in stages. A subtle purple progress mark supplies the motion. The screen leaves substantial dark space around the content.

**Energy and motion.** Quietly anticipatory, technical, and cinematic. The reveal unfolds in measured beats and reads as preparation rather than a generic spinner.

**Harbour use.** Use the staged reveal only for short, real transitions (for example, preparing a local translation result). Show a plain-language status and the actual step underway. For data loading, keep the existing skeleton pattern; do not imply an AI service is working when the feature is local or a sample. Respect reduced motion and provide an immediate static state.

### 6. `login-signup-pageinsp.mp4` — split-panel account flow

**Observed.** A pale canvas holds a split composition: a blue illustrated benefit panel on the left and a white form panel on the right. The left artwork/cards change to explain benefits; the form stays visually stable. At the end, a cobalt processing screen uses a small center point and a gold ring to indicate progress. The typography is clean sans-serif, with generous form spacing and clear field labels.

**Energy and motion.** Welcoming, structured, and reassuring. The visual panel carries the storytelling; the form remains predictable. The processing state is brief and visually distinct from the input screen.

**Harbour use.** Keep Harbour's local-profile truth visible: no account service or password is implied. Use the split layout to show what a profile helps personalize while keeping the form and privacy explanation steady. Only show progress when a real local save is happening; then confirm the saved state. On narrow screens, stack the visual and form without hiding required information.

### 7. `nightmodedashbaord-insp.mp4` — dark analytics dashboard

**Observed.** A near-black/navy dashboard combines a persistent side rail, compact metric cards, a line chart, a lower bar chart, and a purple insight panel. It opens slightly blurred and sharpens; KPI values count up and chart strokes/bars draw into view. Purple is the main highlight, with bright chart colors against dark surfaces.

**Energy and motion.** Focused and analytical. The initial blur-to-sharp reveal gives a clear entrance, while the staged metric and chart motion draws attention to the information hierarchy.

**Harbour use.** Apply the hierarchy and chart choreography to the personal dashboard: checklist activity, locally stored progress, and clear empty states. Keep real values tied to local data; explicitly label demo balances and sample content. Do not create AI insight claims or financial analytics that Harbour does not have. Charts must retain text summaries for screen readers and must not use color as the only key.

### 8. `products-page-translatorins.mp4` — translation product showcase

**Observed.** A dark outer frame contains a product website that changes between light and dark sections. The light opening features a multicolor orbit around a central icon; later panels show feature cards and translation-related visuals, with a pastel closing section and orbiting colored shapes. The visual language combines clean sans-serif UI typography, colorful rounded forms, and dark product panels.

**Energy and motion.** Curious and lively. The website slides into frame, sections change as the page moves, and orbiting objects make the language product feel active and connected.

**Harbour use.** Translate orbiting dots into a language relationship: source phrase, Cantonese characters, jyutping, and a tone note arranged around a central conversation marker. Keep the actual translation readable in a stable card; orbit is decorative and never carries meaning alone. The current translator examples are canned demos and must stay labelled `DEMO` until a real translation capability exists.

## Harbour's visual direction

### Color

Use the references' stronger contrasts as art direction: saturated cobalt/indigo for editorial scenes, emerald/teal for progress and navigation, violet for secondary activity, warm gold for a meaningful highlight, and pale paper or deep ink as alternating section surfaces. Harbour's current token file already provides semantic accent, gold, and five chart colors. Reuse those roles for the first implementation. If the product adopts a broader public-facing palette, define and contrast-check named tokens in `src/styles/tokens.css`; do not scatter literal colors through components. Never use hue alone for risk, status, or completion.

### Typography

Keep the installed Bricolage Grotesque headings, Geist interface/body copy, and Geist Mono utility labels. Let the display face carry a short hero statement; use a restrained italic treatment for one phrase where the chosen font supports it. Use Geist for forms, navigation, explanations, and dashboard figures. Use mono only for compact step numbers, timestamps, and explicit status labels. Do not imitate the reference brands' exact letterforms or copy. Preserve text scaling and reflow.

### Motion principles

- Use motion to explain sequence, location, or state: reveal a section, show a saved profile, draw a chart based on real values, or move translation parts into a readable result.
- Keep everyday content motion short and one-shot. Avoid scroll hijacking, continuous parallax, and auto-advancing carousels.
- A loading animation must name the real operation. Use skeletons for page/data loading and a brief progress treatment only for an operation that is actually in progress.
- Keep the full final content in the DOM. `prefers-reduced-motion` removes movement without removing information.
- Do not place an intro animation in front of emergency numbers, scam warnings, form submission, or other time-sensitive actions.
- Use transforms and opacity for transitions. Do not animate layout dimensions or repeatedly blur large surfaces.

## Review checklist for future motion work

1. Identify the exact Harbour state that triggers the animation and the final state it communicates.
2. Keep the source and any externally inspired artwork licensed, original, or replaced with Harbour-owned art.
3. Check keyboard access, focus visibility, reduced motion, contrast, small-screen layout, and a no-animation rendering.
4. Confirm progress and metrics correspond to actual local work/data; label samples, demos, and simulations.
5. Keep the user's next action available throughout and after the animation.
